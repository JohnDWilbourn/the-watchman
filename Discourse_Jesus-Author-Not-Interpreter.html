The Author Who Entered His Own Text

A Confrontation with the Academic Misframing of Jesus and the Old Testament

August 2026  ·  John David Wilbourn  ·  Christology  ·  Hermeneutics

The Error in Plain Sight

In the 2023 Dictionary of the New Testament Use of the Old Testament — a flagship reference work edited by G.K. Beale, D.A. Carson, Benjamin Gladd, and Andrew Naselli, and named Book of the Year by the Southwestern Journal of Theology — Craig L. Blomberg writes the following about Jesus's citation of Isaiah 29:13 against the Pharisees in Mark 7:

Exhibit — Blomberg, "Jesus's Use of the OT," DNTUOT (Baker Academic, 2023), p. 377
"The OT text is not a future-referring one but an indictment of the Israelites in Isaiah's day. Jesus is interpreting the text typologically, seeing a new recurrence of the same behavior and attitudes in his day."
— Craig L. Blomberg

I caught this the moment I read it. The error is not buried in technical footnotes. It sits on the surface of the prose, fully visible, and it passed through the editorial oversight of four of the most respected evangelical scholars currently working in the field without anyone flagging it. That fact is itself an indictment — not of those men personally, but of the institutional framework that produced the error and is constitutionally unable to see it.

The purpose of this article is to name that error precisely, trace it to its root, identify its most prominent expressions in the current literature, and equip the reader to recognize and sift through it whenever it appears in their studies.

◆

What the Error Actually Claims

When Blomberg writes that Jesus "is interpreting the text typologically," he is placing Jesus inside a hermeneutical tradition as a practitioner of it — a skilled one, a Christocentric one, but a practitioner nonetheless. This is not a minor calibration error. It is a categorical misidentification of Jesus's relationship to the Old Testament.

Jesus did not interpret Isaiah 29:13.
Jesus authored it.

In Mark 7, Jesus is not a first-century exegete identifying a typological pattern. He is the Author of the text specifying, with absolute authority, the identity of its target. When he says "Isaiah was right when he prophesied about you," that is not a hermeneutical claim. That is an authorial disclosure. The difference between those two things is not a matter of degree. It is a difference in kind so fundamental that no amount of sophisticated scholarship can bridge it from the wrong side.

◆

Upstream and Downstream

The prophets are downstream figures. This is not a criticism of their vocation — it is the definition of it. 2 Peter 1:21Loading… states the case plainly: holy men of God spoke as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. They received. They were instruments through whom the Word moved into the human realm. Their personalities were engaged, their language was their own, but the originating will was not theirs. They were downstream by design.

Jesus is upstream. He is the Word before the word is spoken through the prophets. He is the originating will that the prophets receive and transmit. He does not receive the Spirit — he sends the Spirit. He is not in the stream. He is the source.

When any scholar — regardless of their evangelical credentials — places Jesus within the prophetic tradition, even as its ultimate expression, they have moved Jesus from upstream to downstream from the will and acts of God. Everything follows from that repositioning. He becomes a receiver of tradition rather than its originator. A reader of texts rather than their Author. A participant in Israel's story rather than the Author stepping into his own narrative.

1 Peter 1:10–12Loading… closes this case definitively. The prophets searched and inquired carefully concerning the salvation they were predicting. The Spirit active in their inquiry is identified as the Spirit of Christ. The prophets were searching, under the guidance of the Spirit of Christ, into words that the Spirit of Christ had given them to speak. Jesus is on both sides of the prophetic transaction simultaneously — the Author of the oracle and the subject of it. No hermeneutical framework that places Jesus on only one side of that transaction can adequately account for what the text describes.

◆

John's Prologue Saw This Coming

John opens his Gospel the way he does for a reason. He is not borrowing philosophical vocabulary from Hellenistic thought to make Jesus culturally accessible, as the academy frequently argues. He is making a precise Christological declaration that preemptively answers the category errors that scholars are still committing two thousand years later.

In the beginning was the Word. Not: in the beginning, the Word was positioned within Israel's prophetic tradition. Before anything existed to interpret, before any text existed to read, before Israel existed to have a tradition — the Word already was. That is an upstream statement of absolute ontological priority. The deliberate echo of Genesis 1:1 is the opening move of the argument: you cannot place an interpreter before the existence of the texts he interprets.

And the Word was God. Not: the Word was the supreme instance of divine inspiration. The Creator — the one through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made that has been made.

And the Word became flesh. The Creator of matter, space, and time entered the history he controlled and guided. The Author stepped into his own text. The one who spoke Isaiah into existence through a human prophet now stood in a synagogue in Nazareth and read those words aloud, then closed the scroll and said: today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing (Luke 4:20–21Loading…). That is not interpretation. That is the Author arriving at the passage he wrote.

John's prologue is not theological throat-clearing. It is the lens the reader is required to carry through the entire Gospel — and through the entire Old Testament behind it. Every time Jesus cites Scripture in John, the reader who has absorbed the prologue knows they are watching the Word handle his own prior speech. John saw the reductionist error coming. He answered it before it arrived.

◆

The Three Principal Offenders

Craig L. Blomberg is the immediate and documented case. The Beale dictionary article is the exhibit in hand. But the error is not isolated to that piece. In his Gospel Coalition essay "Jesus's View of the Old Testament," Blomberg states explicitly that "like other rabbis, Jesus can recognize some books as more central than others." This is the error in its most concentrated form — Jesus placed in the same hermeneutical category as other rabbis, distinguished perhaps by the quality of his conclusions, but not by a categorically different relationship to the text. The Author of the text reduced to its most competent reader.

N.T. Wright, in Jesus and the Victory of God and throughout the Christian Origins and the Question of God series, frames Jesus as a first-century Jewish figure who "reread," "retold," and "reimagined" Israel's story. Wright's work contains real insight, and his Christological instincts often point in the right direction — he recognizes that Jesus was making divine identity claims that went beyond anything a prophet would say. But his methodology will not allow him to follow those instincts to their conclusion. In Part II of Jesus and the Victory of God, Wright positions Jesus as a prophet-like figure operating within Second Temple Judaism's interpretive world. That framing, however carefully qualified, moves Jesus from upstream to downstream from the will and acts of God. A prophet-like Jesus is a Jesus who follows the script. The actual Jesus wrote it.

Richard Longenecker's Biblical Exegesis in the Apostolic Period (Eerdmans, 1975; revised 1999) is the foundational text for the entire field and the most systematically consequential of the three. Longenecker builds a taxonomy of Jewish exegetical methods — pesher, midrash, allegory, literalism — and places Jesus within it. Jesus is distinguished by his Christocentric perspective, but not by a categorically different relationship to the texts he engages. This assumption, embedded in the foundational reference work of the discipline, shapes every subsequent treatment that builds on it — including Blomberg's dictionary article published nearly fifty years later. The error propagates because it is native to the framework itself.

◆

The Root of the Error

These three men did not arrive at this error independently. They absorbed it from the methodological framework of the academic discipline they were trained in. Historical criticism, source analysis, form criticism, redaction criticism — the entire apparatus was designed by finite creatures to analyze artifacts produced by other finite creatures. It has no category for what happens when the Infinite enters the finite, or when finite creatures attempt to analyze texts authored by the Infinite. Rather than acknowledging the tools' limitation, the field has consistently reframed the object of study to fit the tools.

This produces a fatal inconsistency in institutions that perpetuate it. Verbal plenary inspiration is affirmed in the systematic theology classroom — every word God-breathed, the Spirit as ultimate Author. That same Spirit is then functionally excluded in the hermeneutics classroom, where the historical-critical method takes over without anyone noting the contradiction. A student can affirm divine authorship on Monday and spend Tuesday analyzing Jesus's "exegetical method" as though it belonged to the same category as the methods of Hillel or Shammai. The right hand does not know what the left hand is doing, and the institutional structure of the seminary ensures that it never will.

The Q hypothesis is a symptom of the same exclusion. The documentary source hypothesis exists because scholars operating under historical-critical assumptions could not accept that two independent witnesses could produce similar accounts without a common written source behind them. The framework required a documentary explanation because the actual explanation had been ruled out methodologically in advance. The actual explanation is the Holy Spirit — the same Spirit superintending the same witnesses' testimony of the same events. Q is a gap-filling exercise made necessary by the prior exclusion of the one operative cause that actually explains the data. The field's refusal to grant the Holy Spirit as a real cause in the production of the texts generates a hundred manufactured problems, of which Q is only the most famous.

◆

The Correct Framework

During the incarnation, Jesus operated by deliberate kenotic choice within the Spirit's power rather than the independent exercise of his divine attributes. This means every engagement Jesus had with the Old Testament text was occurring within the same pneumatic operating environment that was delivered to the human realm at Pentecost. The Spirit who had originally authored those texts through the prophets was the same Spirit operating through Jesus as he cited and applied them in real time.

This closes the gap the academic framework can never close. Blomberg, Longenecker, and Wright all struggle in their different ways with the question of how an ancient text's meaning connects to its New Testament application. They cannot bridge that gap because they have excluded the one bridge that actually exists: the Author Spirit operating on both sides of it simultaneously. Original utterance and authoritative application are not separated by centuries of hermeneutical distance. They are unified by the continuous operation of the same Spirit.

The Pentecost connection extends this further. The Spirit-indwelt believer in the Church Age participates in the Author's own operational environment. This is what Paul means in 1 Corinthians 2:14–16Loading… when he states that the spiritual man discerns all things and has the mind of Christ. He is not describing a refined interpretive skill. He is describing participation in the Author's own cognition through the indwelling Spirit — an epistemological ground categorically unavailable to the historical-critical method regardless of how sophisticated its practitioners become.

This is also the nature of the unity of the Body of Christ that Paul extols in 1 Corinthians 12Loading… and Ephesians 4Loading…. The Body's unity is not organizational — it is pneumatological. One Spirit, one baptism, one Body. The mechanism binding believers together is the same Spirit who authored the texts, who operated through the incarnate Son, who was delivered at Pentecost, and who now indwells every member. The Body shares participation in the Author's operating environment. That shared participation is what makes genuine engagement with the Word possible, and what no academic credential can replicate or substitute for.

◆

What These Scholars Have Done to Jesus

At worst — Blomberg's framework — Jesus is an academic. A skilled exegete employing recognizable Jewish hermeneutical methods, distinguishable from a competent first-century rabbi only by the authority of his conclusions. The Author of everything placed on the same plane as the creatures he made, whose cognitive capacity he designed.

At best — Wright's framework — Jesus is the ultimate prophet. A prophet however great is still a creature receiving and transmitting. Jesus is the one the prophets received from.

Neither position is wrong by a matter of degree. They are wrong in kind. The difference between Jesus as interpreter and Jesus as the Logos through whom all things were made is not a difference on a continuous scale. No point on the human scale connects to it. Every category these scholars deploy — prophet, exegete, interpreter, reader, rabbi — belongs to the realm of what was made. Jesus belongs to the realm of the Maker.

The Creator of matter, space, and time entered the history he controlled and guided, to defeat the devil, to pay the personal penalty for human sin, and to inaugurate a kingdom populated by subsequent believers in his mission. There is no social analogue for this. Every human category breaks under its weight. Conqueror — falls short. King — falls short. Founder — falls short. Prophet — falls furthest short of all.

Thomas understood the ceiling of human language on this subject and gave the only adequate response available to a finite creature beholding the Infinite: My Lord and my God (John 20:28Loading…). That is what the academic framework, at its best, cannot reach — and at its worst, actively obscures.

◆

A Word to the Reader

If you are engaging the serious academic literature on Jesus and the Old Testament — and you should, because the observational labor these scholars produce is genuinely valuable even when their framework is wrong — you now have the instrument to sift it correctly. The Romans Road Commentary, at 1.3 million words of verse-by-verse doctrinal exegesis, exists precisely to provide the receipts that validate a framework anchored in what Scripture actually claims about itself and its Author.

Receive the data. Note the citations, the patterns, the intertextual connections these scholars catalog. That work is real and useful. But whenever you encounter the language of Jesus interpreting, reading, employing, applying, rereading, retelling, or reimagining the Old Testament texts — stop. That language is carrying a framework that misidentifies Jesus's relationship to those texts at the most fundamental level.

Jesus does not interpret the Old Testament. He authored it. Every engagement he has with those texts in the Gospel narratives is the Author specifying, with absolute authority, the meaning of his own prior words. That is not interpretation. That is disclosure.

The canon is now closed. The words of God incarnate, continuous with every prophetic oracle he authored through the Spirit before the incarnation, collected and transmitted by faithful Jewish eyewitnesses under that same Spirit's superintendence, constitute the complete and sufficient Word of God. Nothing is missing. Nothing awaits addition. The Spirit who authored the text now indwells every believer in the Body of Christ, providing the operational environment within which that complete Word is correctly engaged.

You do not need the academy's permission to see what is plainly there. You need the Spirit who put it there.
