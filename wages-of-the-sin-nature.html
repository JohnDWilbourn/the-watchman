The Wages of the Sin Nature and the Spiritual Life

I. The Text and Its Audience

"For the wages of sin is death." Romans 6:23a

This statement is routinely deployed in evangelistic contexts as a declaration of divine judgment against the unbeliever. That application is not wrong. But it is incomplete, and incompleteness at the level of audience identification produces a different sermon than the one Paul is actually preaching.

Romans 6 is not addressed to unbelievers. It is addressed to believers. The governing question of the chapter is Paul's own: "Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?" That is not an evangelistic question. It is a sanctification question, directed at people already in Christ, already indwelt by the Holy Spirit, already possessing eternal life. The wages of sin Paul has in view are collecting on believers — right now, in this life — not exclusively on the unbeliever at the Great White Throne.

The Greek word translated wages is ὀψώνια (opsōnia) — a military payroll term. What a soldier is owed. What has been earned and is therefore contractually due. Sin pays out exactly what it owes. The believer who walks in the sin nature is not exempt from that payroll. He receives his wages in the currency of the carnal state.

II. The Wages in the Believer's Life

What does the sin nature pay out to the believer who operates under its control?

  • Broken fellowship with God — the immediate consequence of any sin committed by the believer
  • Carnality — the sustained condition of a believer out of fellowship, described in 1 Corinthians 3 as an infant, producing wood, hay, and stubble
  • Divine discipline — the Father's corrective response to the sinning believer, Hebrews 12, framed explicitly within the Father-son relationship
  • Loss of rewards — 1 Corinthians 3:15, the believer whose life's work burns at the Bema; saved, but so as through fire
  • Physical death as extreme discipline — 1 Corinthians 11:30 and 1 John 5:16, the sin unto death

Paul's statement in Romans 8:6 is unambiguous: "For to set the mind on the flesh is death." Not leads toward. Not resembles. Is. Present tense. A living, indwelt, eternally secure believer functioning in the sin nature experiences the operational reality of death — spiritually inert, incapable of producing divine good, cut off from the fellowship that constitutes the Christian life.

This is the wages landing on the believer in real time. The payroll runs every day the believer remains out of fellowship.

III. The Mechanism of Restoration

The wages are real. The mechanism of restoration is equally real and equally simple.

"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." 1 John 1:9

This is the first and foundational Problem-Solving Device: Rebound. Naming the sin to God — not to a priest, not to a pastor, not to a congregation — restores fellowship instantly. The Greek verb confess (ὁμολογέω) means to say the same thing, to agree with God's assessment of the sin. No emotional performance required. No penance. No public declaration. One act of the will, directed toward God, and fellowship is restored.

Without this mechanism the believer has no way back. He may be genuinely saved, genuinely indwelt, genuinely secure — and functionally dead. Walking in the valley of the shadow of death. The Shepherd is present — you are with me — the security is unbroken. But the green pastures and still waters are not where he is.

Rebound as the operational prerequisite for the full know–reckon–yield sequence — the on/off switch for the entire system — is examined in the Illuminations study: Romans 6:11–13 — Know, Reckon, Yield.

IV. The Indwelling Spirit: Capacity, Not Product

The Holy Spirit indwells every true believer at the moment of salvation — not as a reward for spiritual performance, not as a response to consecration, but as a permanent and unconditional consequence of regeneration. This is positional. Fixed. Not subject to the believer's behavior.

But indwelling does not produce growth automatically. The Spirit's presence creates the capacity for growth. It does not produce growth independent of the believer's intake of Bible doctrine under a qualified pastor-teacher.

"Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth." John 17:17

The Spirit is the teacher. But there must be material to teach. The Spirit illuminates doctrine; He does not replace it. A believer with the Spirit resident but no doctrine in the soul is like a student in a classroom with a brilliant instructor and no textbook. The capacity for learning is present. The learning is not happening.

The pattern established in Exodus with the manna is not incidental. Daily. Fresh. Could not be stored. Had to be gathered and metabolized continuously. The Lord's prayer encodes the same model — daily bread. Not weekly. Not in a forty-minute pre-packaged Sunday morning format. The biological necessity of daily intake is the template for doctrinal intake.

V. The Shepherd and the Flock

Ezekiel 34 is the prophetic indictment of shepherds who feed themselves while the flock starves. The sheep are scattered. They are weak. They are diseased. And they are not filing complaints — because they have no reference point for what a fed flock looks like.

A congregation that has never received systematic, verse-by-verse, doctrine-driven teaching does not know it is malnourished. It has normalized the hunger. The programs, the emotional experiences, the soul-winning activities, the revival meetings — these register as spiritual life because they are the only spiritual environment the congregation has ever inhabited. There is no contrast class. No reference point for what it feels like when the edification complex of the soul is actually being built.

The shepherd, meanwhile, is in the Word by professional necessity — sermon preparation, Sunday school material, midweek content. He grows, incidentally, as a byproduct of his role. The job forces exposure to the text. He accumulates, week by week, a doctrinal foundation that his congregation never receives in transferable form. What reaches the congregation is not raw doctrine. It is a processed by-product — application-heavy, illustration-heavy, emotionally accessible content from which the doctrine that generated the application has been refined out in the sermon preparation process.

The congregation receives the gravy. The doctrine that produced it never arrives at the table.

And the pastor reads this not as a failure of discipleship but as a methodology preference. Verse-by-verse systematic doctrine is, in his framework, one valid approach among several — a stylistic choice, like one carpenter preferring a hammer and another preferring a nail gun. He cannot see that it is not a preference. It is a biological necessity. Hebrews 5 makes the argument directly: the mature handle solid food; the immature are on milk. This is not a style distinction. It is a developmental requirement.

The congregation is malnourished. The pastor is calling it a diet.

VI. The Two Gospels

At the heart of the confusion between salvation and sanctification lies a failure to distinguish two distinct but sequential gospels.

The gospel of salvation is simple. It has one condition: faith. Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ — not faith plus works, not faith plus Lordship submission, not faith plus behavioral transformation. Faith alone in Christ alone. The transaction is instantaneous. The moment the sinner believes, justification occurs — complete, permanent, not subject to revision or revocation. The righteousness of Christ is imputed. Eternal life is received. The Holy Spirit takes up permanent residence.

The gospel of doctrine is comprehensive. It is the content of the entire spiritual life that follows salvation. It is the mechanism by which the justified believer grows toward spiritual maturity, builds the edification complex of the soul, develops capacity for divine viewpoint, and begins to experience in his daily life what is already permanently true of his position in Christ.

These two gospels are not contradictory. They are sequential. The first gets the believer in the door in an instant. The second is the rest of his life.

Conflating them produces two catastrophic errors simultaneously. It loads conditions onto salvation that do not belong there, corrupting the simplicity of the transaction. And it leaves the already-saved believer without a coherent framework for the spiritual life, because the teacher has confused which conversation he is having.

VII. Positional and Experiential: The Governing Continuum

The foundational grid for understanding the Christian life is the distinction between what is positional and what is experiential.

Positional reality is what God has done for and in the believer that is complete, permanent, and not subject to the believer's behavior. Union with Christ. Imputed righteousness. Eternal security. Indwelling of the Spirit. These are fixed in eternity. They do not fluctuate with the believer's performance. They are true of the carnal believer as surely as they are true of the mature believer. Position is not earned and cannot be lost.

Experiential reality is what the believer actually lives through in time — variable, responsive to volition, subject to the sin nature's influence, the arena in which doctrine either operates or doesn't. Fellowship is experiential in this sense: a status that changes. In fellowship or out. Binary. The mechanism of rebound moves the believer from the experiential reality of carnality back into the experiential reality of fellowship and Spirit-filling.

These two realities operate simultaneously. The believer is always fully positioned in Christ. He is not always in fellowship. Both statements are true at the same moment. Neither cancels the other. The entire spiritual life is the progressive process of allowing the fixed positional truth to govern and inform the variable experiential moment.

"So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus." Romans 6:11

Not become dead to sin. Reckon — λογίζομαι — calculate, take into account as established fact, let the positional reality function as the operating assumption of the experiential moment. The mechanics of sanctification are grounded in the settled facts of justification.

Reckon is the second stage of a three-part operational sequence — know, reckon, yield — rooted in the military and accounting vocabulary of the Roman world. The full lexical, grammatical, and doctrinal treatment of all three stages is in the Illuminations study: Romans 6:11–13 — Know, Reckon, Yield.

VIII. Lordship Salvation: The Category Error

No doctrinal error in contemporary evangelicalism more precisely illustrates the confusion of positional and experiential categories than Lordship salvation.

The position holds that genuine saving faith necessarily includes submission to Christ as Lord — that behavioral transformation, repentance from sin, and ongoing obedience are either components of saving faith or its inevitable fruit. If those evidences are absent, the faith was not genuine. The implication is that carnality in a believer is either temporary or diagnostic of a salvation that never occurred.

The exegetical problem is 1 Corinthians 3. Paul describes the carnal believer in explicit terms: an infant in Christ, out of fellowship, producing wood, hay, and stubble. At the Bema his works burn. He is saved, but only as through fire (v.15). This is not a temporary blip. Paul treats carnality as a recognizable, sustained condition of a genuinely saved person. Lordship salvation has no coherent category for this individual. He must be either temporarily carnal — with no timeline provided — or unsaved, which contradicts Paul's explicit statement.

Furthermore, the historical and exegetical sequence of Christ's Lordship matters. Philippians 2:9-11 — the title Lord of lords and King of kings is the post-resurrection conferral by the Father in response to the humiliation and strategic victory of the cross. Lordship salvation imports this post-resurrection title back into the salvation transaction and requires the sinner to recognize and submit to a position Christ had not yet formally assumed at the point of the cross. The sequence is inverted.

The Noble Confession — Jesus is Lord — is real. The Lordship of Christ is real. Confessing that Lordship is the natural expression of a soul filled with doctrine and the Holy Spirit. But it is fruit, not root. It is the product of a mature spiritual life expressing itself, not the entrance credential for the salvation transaction.

Romans 10:9 — "if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord" — is the text most frequently deployed in defense of Lordship salvation. But Paul's statement in the same breath is definitive: "with the heart one believes and is justified" (v.10). The belief precedes the confession. The heart transaction precedes the verbal expression. The sequence is in the text itself.

Making the confession a condition of salvation is doctrinal gatekeeping — taking a true statement, relocating it from its proper domain into the salvation transaction where it cannot function without corrupting the mechanism, and erecting it as a threshold the sinner must clear before the grace of God is permitted to operate. It turns the simplicity of coming as a child into an entrance examination with no defined passing score and a gatekeeper who can always move the goalposts.

IX. Doctrine as Food, Not Gate

Real doctrine is not a gate. It is food.

You do not stand at the kitchen door requiring the hungry person to demonstrate that they deserve the meal. You feed them, and the strength that follows is the evidence that the food was real. The sequence is: receive, metabolize, produce. Not: demonstrate capacity to receive, demonstrate prerequisite commitment, then receive.

Ephesians 4 — the pastor-teacher equipping the saints for the work of ministry — is post-salvation infrastructure. The equipping assumes the saints are already saints. The work of the pastor-teacher begins after the salvation transaction, not as a condition of it.

The product of metabolized doctrine is not religious performance visible from the outside. It is stability under pressure. Peace that passes understanding. Response to adversity that makes no sense by the metrics of human wisdom.

"I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content." Philippians 4:11

Not a doctrine about peace. Actual peace operating in an actual Roman prison, in a man who locates the source of that contentment not in circumstances but in the sufficiency of Christ mediated through a lifetime of doctrinal intake.

X. The Conduit and the Gatekeeper

The legitimate role of the pastor-teacher is Spirit-dependent transmission.

He has done the work — the languages, the exegesis, the systematic categories, the isagogics, the historical context. The Spirit illuminates his teaching to the prepared human spirits of his congregation. The believer's human spirit receives and metabolizes the content. The pastor is a conduit. His authority is derived entirely from the accuracy of the content and the Spirit's work in the hearer. He is not in the loop beyond faithful delivery.

This model produces spiritually mature, independent believers who can open the Word themselves. Who carry the categories internally. Who do not require the pastor's framework to interpret their experience. Whose spiritual life is genuinely theirs because the doctrine has been genuinely metabolized.

The corrupted model substitutes a control mechanism for the transmission model. The pastor repositions himself from conduit to gatekeeper. The philosophical interpretation is his. The definition of yieldedness is his. The threshold of acceptable submission is his. The Spirit is effectively excluded because the transaction is now between the believer and the pastor's system rather than between the believer and the living Word illuminated by the living Spirit.

The tell is the word yieldedness. It sounds spiritual. It sounds humble. But what it is demanding, beneath the theological vocabulary, is submission to the man and his system dressed in the language of submission to God.

One model builds the edification complex of the soul in the believer.

The other builds the pastor's empire.

The vocabulary is identical. The spirit is opposite.

Conclusion

The wages of the sin nature are paid out daily to believers who have never been taught the mechanism that stops the payroll. They are busy. They are devoted. They hand out tracts on Saturday morning, volunteer in the community, support missionaries, fill the pews faithfully, and run entirely on the energy of the sin nature — because no one handed them the one verse, the one act, the one Problem-Solving Device that would have changed everything.

The Holy Spirit is resident. The capacity for growth is present. The positional reality is fixed and eternal and glorious. And the experiential reality is a valley — not because God has abandoned the believer, but because the shepherd never fed the flock, and the flock never knew it was hungry, and the sheep followed because that is what sheep do.

The gospel of salvation is simple enough for a child. Come. Believe. Receive.

The gospel of doctrine is comprehensive enough for a lifetime.

The tragedy is not that people cannot understand the distinction. The tragedy is that almost no one is teaching it.
